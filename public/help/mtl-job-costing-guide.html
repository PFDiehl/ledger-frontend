What job costing does

Normal bookkeeping tells you how the whole business is doing. Job costing tells you how each project is doing — so you can see which jobs make money and which quietly lose it.

The idea is simple: you create a job for each project, then tag the invoices, bills, expenses, and bank charges that belong to it. MTL adds up the money in and the money out and shows you the profit on that job.

You'll find it under Jobs in the left sidebar (under the "Job costing" heading).

Tagging is always optional. Anything you don't tag just stays in your regular books exactly as before — turning on job costing changes nothing about how the rest of MTL works.
Step one

Create a job

  1. Click Jobs in the sidebar, then + New job.
  2. Give it a name (e.g. "Smith Residence — Kitchen Remodel"). Everything else is optional.
  3. Add a job number, customer, and — if you want estimate-vs-actual — your estimated revenue (the contract amount) and estimated cost (your budget).
  4. Save. The job starts as Active; you can mark it Completed or Archived later.
Good to know: the job pickers throughout MTL only appear once you've created at least one job — so the rest of your screens stay uncluttered if you don't use job costing.
Step two

Tag money to a job

Every place money enters your books now has a Job picker. Tag an item to a job and it counts toward that job's profit. You can tag as you go, or assign things in bulk from inside the job.

Quote vs. reality

Estimate vs. actual

If you entered an estimated revenue and estimated cost when you created the job, the job page also compares your quote to how it's actually tracking — your estimated profit, your actual profit so far, and the difference. It's the fastest way to catch a job drifting over budget while you can still do something about it.

No estimate yet? You can add one anytime — open the job, click Edit, and fill in the estimated revenue and cost.
The one rule

Tag each cost once

Job costing shows you exactly what's tagged — it doesn't guess. So the one thing to get right: tag each dollar in only one place.

For example, if you buy $800 of lumber on a card, you can either enter it as a bill or expense or tag the bank charge for it — but not both, or that $800 gets counted as $1,600 of cost on the job.

Rule of thumb: most trades tag costs in one spot — usually right on the bank "For review" row as charges come in. Pick the spot that fits your workflow and stick to it.
At a glance

Quick answers

A cost looks doubled on a job.

It's probably tagged in two places — e.g. both as an expense and on the bank charge that paid it. Remove the job tag from one of them (open the job → Remove, or clear the Job picker on the item).

Can I move an item to a different job?

Yes — just change its Job picker to the other job, or re-assign it from inside the job. Nothing is locked.

If I delete a job, do I lose the invoices and costs?

No. Deleting a job keeps all the invoices, bills, and expenses — they simply lose the job tag and stay in your regular books.

My profit number looks off.

Two usual causes: something's double-tagged (see above), or a cost/revenue item just isn't tagged to the job yet. Open the job to see exactly what's counted, and use + Assign to pull in anything missing.

Do I have to use job costing for every company?

No. It's entirely optional and per-company. If you never create a job, nothing changes and no job pickers appear.
