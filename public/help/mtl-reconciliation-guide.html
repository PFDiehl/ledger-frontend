Reconciliation

Prove your books match the bank — the monthly check that makes every report you run trustworthy.

A guide for business owners & bookkeepers
The idea

Matching your books to the bank

Your books are your record of what happened with your money. The bank statement is the bank's record of the same thing. Reconciliation is the monthly habit of laying the two side by side and confirming they agree — to the penny.

If they match, you've proven your books are accurate for that period. If they don't, something is missing, duplicated, or entered wrong — and reconciliation is how you catch it. It's the most important check a bookkeeper does, because every report (profit, taxes, how much cash you really have) is only trustworthy once the books have been reconciled.

Why they differ

Two balances, one truth

At any moment there are two balances: what the bank says you have, and what your books say you have. They're usually a little apart — a check you wrote hasn't cleared yet, a deposit is still in transit, or a transaction hasn't been entered. None of that is an error by itself; reconciliation is how you account for the gap and prove the rest is right.

See it at a glance: on the Banking page, each account shows a Bank balance (what the bank reports) above the balance In MountainTop Ledger (what's recorded), plus a count of items still waiting to be reviewed. Reconciliation is how you close that gap on purpose.
In MountainTop Ledger

How to reconcile, step by step

  1. On the Banking page, select the account and click Reconcile.
  2. Enter two figures from your bank statement: the statement ending date and the statement ending balance. MTL fills in the beginning balance for you — it's the ending balance from your last reconciliation (or you type it in the first time, which is your opening balance).
  3. MTL lists every transaction on that account that hasn't been reconciled yet.
  4. Check off each transaction that also appears on your bank statement — the ones that have cleared the bank.
  5. As you check items, a running tally updates at the bottom: beginning balance, plus deposits cleared, minus payments cleared, equals your cleared balance — along with a Difference (your statement's ending balance minus that cleared balance).
  6. When the Difference reaches $0.00, your books and the bank agree. Click Finish reconciliation.
What "finish" does: MTL saves a snapshot of the reconciliation (the date, the balances, and which transactions cleared) and locks those transactions so they can't be quietly changed later — that's what protects a closed month. You can print a report of what was reconciled, reprint past ones, or undo a reconciliation if a statement was entered wrong.
Troubleshooting

When it won't reach $0.00

That's reconciliation doing its job. A leftover difference almost always means one of three things:

A transaction is on your statement but not in MTL — add it.
A transaction is in MTL but not on the statement — it may be wrong or a duplicate.
An amount doesn't match — correct it. Keep adjusting until the difference is $0.00.
Good to know

Two things specific to MTL

Reconciliation is done per bank account, and it only sees transactions that exist as bank transactions — from the bank feed, an import, the Banking page's Add a transaction, or an account register. If a manual item isn't showing up to reconcile, that's the usual reason.

A split transaction appears as a single line that matches the one amount on your statement, while the category breakdown is kept in the books behind it — so your reconciliation stays clean and your reporting stays detailed.
