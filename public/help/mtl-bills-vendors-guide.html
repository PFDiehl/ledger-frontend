The people you pay

Vendors

A vendor is anyone you pay — suppliers, subcontractors, your landlord. Vendors are the money-out side of your contacts, just like customers are the money-in side.

Open Vendors to add one, or they're created automatically the first time you enter a bill for them.

What you owe

Enter a bill

A bill is money you owe and will pay later. (Already paid it? Use an expense instead.)

  1. Open Bills → New bill.
  2. Enter the vendor, amount, due date, and a category (from your chart of accounts).
  3. For job costing, pick a Job. Save.
Why track bills: they show you what's coming due so nothing slips, and they power your accounts-payable and cash-flow views.
When you pay

Mark a bill paid

  1. Open the bill and choose Mark as Paid.
  2. Set the paid date — the day the money actually went out. That date drives your cash-basis reporting.
Tip: if the payment came through a connected bank, you can match the bank charge to the bill in Banking instead — that marks it paid and reconciles it in one step.
Year-end

1099 contractors

If you pay independent contractors, you may need to file a 1099 for each one you paid $600 or more in a year. MTL tracks this for you.

  1. Mark the vendor as a 1099 contractor and note whether you have their W-9 on file.
  2. Through the year, their payments add up automatically.
  3. At year-end, open Reports → Contractors (1099) to see each contractor, their total, and whether they crossed the $600 threshold.
Collect W-9s early. It's far easier to get a contractor's W-9 when you first hire them than to chase it down the following January.

Quick answers

What's the difference between a bill and an expense?

A bill is owed and paid later (accounts payable). An expense is already paid. Both let you pick a category and a job.

A subcontractor's cost should hit a specific job.

Pick that Job on the bill (or on the bank charge that pays it). It then counts as a cost on that project.

Does MTL file the 1099s for me?

MTL tracks the totals and flags who needs one. Use the Contractors (1099) report to prepare and file through your usual process.
