Where to find them

Open Reports from the sidebar. Across the top you'll see tabs for each report: P&L, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow, Aged A/R, Trial Balance, General Ledger, and Contractors (1099).

Here's what the three you'll use most actually mean.

Am I making money?

Profit & Loss (P&L)

Your income minus your expenses over a period — the bottom line is your profit (or loss).

It answers "did the business make money last month / last quarter / this year?" Income is grouped at the top, expenses below, and the difference is your net profit.

Cash vs. accrual: on the P&L you can switch the basis. Cash counts income and expenses when money actually moves; accrual counts them when they're earned or incurred. Cash is simplest for most small businesses.
What am I worth?

Balance Sheet

A snapshot, as of a date, of what you own (assets), what you owe (liabilities), and what's left over (equity).

Where the P&L covers a stretch of time, the Balance Sheet is a single moment — "where do things stand today?" Assets always equal liabilities plus equity; that's the balance.

Where did the cash go?

Cash Flow

How cash actually moved in and out over a period — which can differ from profit.

You can be "profitable" on paper but short on cash (money tied up in unpaid invoices), or the reverse. Cash flow shows the real money movement, so you can see whether you'll be able to cover what's coming.

The rest

The other four

ReportAnswers
Aged A/RWho owes you money and how overdue it is (30 / 60 / 90+ days). Your collections to-do list.
Trial BalanceEvery account's balance in one list — an accountant's check that the books balance.
General LedgerEvery transaction posted to a given account, in detail. Use it to trace exactly what makes up a number.
Contractors (1099)Each 1099 contractor's total for the year and whether they crossed $600 (see the Bills, Vendors & 1099s guide).
Fine-tuning

Periods & options

  • Period: view a full year, a single quarter (Q1–Q4), or a custom date range.
  • Compare: on the P&L you can compare against the prior period to see what changed.
  • Basis & filters: switch cash/accrual on the P&L, or narrow some reports to a specific account.

Quick answers

Which report should I look at most?

The P&L for "am I making money," and Cash Flow or your bank balance for "do I have money." Together they tell the real story.

My profit looks high but my account is low.

Classic timing gap — profit counts invoices you've billed, cash counts what's actually arrived. Check Aged A/R for unpaid invoices.

An accountant asked for my trial balance.

It's a tab right in Reports — open it and you can share or export it.
